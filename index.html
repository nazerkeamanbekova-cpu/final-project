    Qoldau AI · A research proposal · Kazakhstan

    One citizen. One request. How many government departments?

    Research question

    What are the results of using AI-assisted classification and routing for the administrative treatment of citizens’ requests in Kazakhstan, and which institutional, linguistic, and ethical barriers can hinder this process?

    Qoldau AI proposed suggests the right office; a public servant decides.

    The problem

    When a request has to find its owner

    In plain words: When you send a complaint to the government, it is sometimes passed from office to office before the right one deals with it. This happens more and more.

    5M+applications received by eOtinish in 2025Over 4M from individuals, ~1M from businesses · (Ministry, 2026)
    16.2M+appeals processed since launch(Ministry, 2026)
    26,000+organisations connected(Ministry, 2026)
    380,000+employees involved in processing appeals(Ministry, 2026)

    Redirected requests keep rising

    Redirected requests rose 54.5% in a year, from about 1.1 million to over 1.7 million.

    In 2025: 173,626 redirects in Almaty’s akimat, 70,192 in Astana’s (Gov. of Kazakhstan, 2026, p. 12).

    Careful: a redirect is not always an error. The data cannot separate legitimate transfers from avoidable ones.

    Not every redirect is a failure

    legitimate

    Another body has the authority

    Art. 65 allows up to 3 working days to transfer an appeal to the competent body.

    possibly avoidable

    Wrong first destination

    More steps, slower answers, more staff load. How often it happens is not public.

    measured, but narrow

    Redirected too late

    Over 20,000 of 2.7 million requests in H1 2025 (Gov. of Kazakhstan, 2026).

    Citizens

    Transfers slow down urgent housing, social and safety issues.

    Public servants

    Sorting cases takes effort that could go to solving them.

    Accountability

    What matters is not how many requests arrive, but whether they reach the right office and get answered.

      Repeated transfers

      Each handover starts a new review and a new notification to the applicant.

      More administrative work

      Staff re-read, re-classify and re-forward instead of assessing the substance.

      Uncertainty for citizens

      The applicant cannot tell who now owns the problem — or how long it will take.

      How it works — click a step

      Rules + learning

      Machine learning plus an up-to-date database of who is responsible for what.

      Automatic flags

      Low confidence, unclear responsibility or urgency: a human takes a second look.

      What it will not do

      It would not reject appeals, decide eligibility or make final decisions.

      possible extension — not in the paper

      Grouping similar requests

      Link many reports of the same problem (e.g. one building without heating) into one case. An operator would confirm every merge; each citizen still gets a reply. Untested.

      Nationally coordinated, locally operated

      A national body sets standards and monitors performance; local akimats integrate it and supervise operators.

      Why iKOMEK109?

      A controlled pilot inside an existing system, not a new channel. iKOMEK109 already handles over 2.2 million appeals a year (Ministry, 2026; Astana City Akimat, 2025).

      The risks

      What could go wrong?

      Click a card. These are risks to investigate, not proven findings.

      In plain words: The AI can make mistakes, may work worse in one language, and people may trust it too much. So safeguards are needed.

      Why human review must be more than a rubber stamp

      People tend to trust AI advice that confirms what they already think (Selten et al., 2023). Oversight can become symbolic.

      Design safeguards proposed in the paper

      Try it yourself · not a forecast

      Would Qoldau AI pay for itself?

      Nobody knows yet how much time it would save. The pilot would measure sliders 1–3.

      Costs are from the paper. Sliders 1–3 are guesses, not data.

      How this is calculated
      1. Hours freed per year = (70,192 × needless passes stopped % × minutes ÷ 60) + (2,200,000 × seconds ÷ 3,600)
      2. Value per year = hours freed × cost of one hour
      3. Net gain per year = value per year − 33.4 million (yearly cost)
      4. Payback time = 80.85 million (trial cost) ÷ net gain per year (only if the net gain is above zero)
      5. Total after N years = −80.85 million + N × net gain per year (the line on the chart)
      6. “Workers’ time” = hours freed ÷ 1,920 (1,920 = 160 working hours a month × 12; my assumption)
      Freed time is not cash or jobs to cut. The paper says Qoldau AI must not be used to cut staff.

      Work changes, it doesn’t vanish

      Less categorising, more complex cases; new work for data and security staff.

      Hidden labour: annotation

      Past requests must be labelled by bilingual experts. It is budgeted, not free.

      Continuous monitoring

      Responsibilities change, so the model needs ongoing checks.

      The verdict

      Should Kazakhstan adopt Qoldau AI?

      In plain words: Try a small trial that is checked by outsiders. Expand only if it clearly works better than today.

      Not as an autonomous system, and not as a reason to cut staff. Yes — to a limited, independently evaluated pilot inside iKOMEK109, under six conditions.

      Lawful, anonymised dataLegally obtained and anonymised training data.
      Two languages, two scoresSeparate evaluation of Kazakh and Russian performance.
      Meaningful human oversightOperators can correct; disagreements are recorded.
      Local data, legal complianceLocal data storage; government-controlled or legally compliant infrastructure; the AI Law’s risk classification set by the responsible authority.
      Transparent procurementOpen, documented purchasing of the system and services.
      Public reportingIndependent monitoring and public reporting of errors, processing times and outcomes.
      if the pilot shows

      Measurable improvement over existing operator decisions: fewer unnecessary transfers, less delay, less repetitive work — without new inequalities or weaker accountability.

      →
      otherwise

      Expansion would not be justified. The paper sets this as an explicit stopping condition.

      Legal context cited in the paper: Law on Artificial Intelligence (Law No. 230-VIII, 17 Nov 2025, Arts. 17–21: risk-based approach, audits incl. training-data quality, transparency); Law on Personal Data and Their Protection (Law No. 94-V, 2013, Art. 12: storage in databases located in Kazakhstan); and the Ministry’s 2026 guidelines on government use of AI (anonymisation, verification of outputs, officials responsible for final decisions).

      The goal is not to automate government for its own sake. It is to make existing public institutions more responsive and accountable to citizens.

      References & methodology

      What the evidence can — and cannot — establish

      In plain words: Where the numbers come from, and what we still do not know.

      Data the project uses

      • eOtinish / Ministry reporting and the 2025 Openness Index
      • Administrative statistics compiled by Beisenov (2025)
      • BNS–UNICEF MICS 2024 household survey
      • Project reports for Bağcılar and Singapore
      • Academic literature on digital government, discretion and text classification

      What it can establish

      • Requests and redirects are growing, and routing is a large part of the workload
      • Automated classification of citizen text is technically feasible, including with Kazakhstan data
      • Kazakhstan has substantial digital infrastructure to build on
      • Benefits of AI are conditional on institutions, oversight and data

      What it cannot establish

      • Whether a given redirect was legitimate, avoidable or slow
      • Whether systems work equally for Kazakh and Russian submissions
      • Whether older, disabled or less digitally literate people face more difficulty
      • That Qoldau AI would reach any particular accuracy, or save any time or money

      Data needed to evaluate Qoldau AI

      • Anonymised request texts with original and final classification
      • Department(s) assigned, transfer histories, processing times, final outcomes
      • Results reported separately for Kazakh and Russian
      • Operator corrections and disagreements with AI output
